Clinical focus areas

These areas are considered after suitability, consent, funding pathway and availability are clarified.

Neurological rehabilitation

Support for people living with stroke, ABI, TBI, spinal cord injury, Parkinsonism, MS and other neurological conditions affecting mobility, balance, endurance and function.

Progressive neurological conditions

Monitoring and therapy planning for changing function, fatigue, respiratory considerations, mobility decline, equipment progression and care-team education.

Complex disability support

Physiotherapy input for people with high support needs where positioning, routines, transfers, equipment, support-worker education and risk management need to be considered together.

Practical needs

What may be reviewed

A focused view of common therapy priorities for mobile neurological and complex disability referrals.

Mobility, transfers and falls risk

Assessment and therapy for walking, transfer safety, mobility aids, access barriers, falls risk and confidence with movement.

Respiratory and fatigue-related support

Support for pacing, airway clearance education, cough assist coordination, respiratory monitoring and escalation planning where clinically appropriate.

Carer and support-worker training

Practical education to help carers and support workers assist with mobility, exercise routines, transfers, positioning and risk awareness.

Function-linked documentation

Reports and recommendations may be provided where clinically appropriate, linking impairments, function, risk, goals and therapy recommendations.

Equipment-related input

Input may include mobility aids, access barriers, positioning, standing, transfer setup and practical progression where clinically appropriate.

Care-team guidance

Communication with relevant family members, coordinators, case managers and treating teams can occur when consent is in place.

Not sure which service fits?

Many referrals involve more than one clinical focus area. A brief suitability check can help clarify whether Tandem Physio is a reasonable fit before detailed information is shared.

Useful starting details

  • Client suburb and funding pathway.
  • Main functional concern or recent change.
  • Known risks, urgency and access issues.
  • Whether reports, training or equipment input may be needed.
