Clinical focus areas

These areas are considered after suitability, consent, funding pathway and availability are clarified.

Stroke

Rehabilitation for mobility, balance, transfers and daily function after stroke.

Motor neurone disease (MND)

Review of changing function, fatigue, respiratory needs, equipment and care-team education.

Spinal cord injury

Mobility, transfers, positioning and equipment needs in daily routines.

Acquired and traumatic brain injury

Physical rehabilitation shaped around mobility, fatigue and support needs.

Multiple sclerosis (MS)

Movement, pacing and equipment strategies for variable or changing capacity.

Parkinson’s and Parkinsonism

Walking, balance and everyday movement in the home and community.

Falls risk

Assessment of balance, mobility aids, transfers and environmental barriers.

Complex chronic pain

Pacing, graded activity and self-management where persistent pain limits daily function and home assessment is clinically appropriate.

Complex needs and care-team support

For people with high support needs, assessment considers positioning, transfers, equipment, routines and risk together. Where clinically appropriate, therapy, carer training and reports link to function and goals; communication with family and care teams requires consent.

Respiratory input may include airway clearance education, cough assist coordination, monitoring, pacing and escalation planning where clinically appropriate.

See the mobile physiotherapy approach for assessment, equipment and training details.
