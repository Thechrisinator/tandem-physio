Persistent pain and function · Western Sydney and The Hills

Rebuild practical activity when persistent pain limits daily life

Tandem considers mobile physiotherapy for adults whose persistent pain is associated with meaningful mobility, functional or disability-related limitations. The focus is on achievable activity and participation where assessment at home is clinically appropriate.

Start with what matters

A goal can be getting through an ordinary task

Preparing a meal, walking to the letterbox or attending an appointment may take considerable planning when pain affects movement. Assessment explores the task, current capacity, symptom pattern, disability-related needs and what the person would like to change.

Pain is real, and its intensity alone does not tell us the amount of tissue damage. Physical, health and life factors can influence a person’s experience. Rehabilitation aims to improve function and confidence without promising that pain will disappear.

Read About Chris and Tandem’s Clinical Focus to understand the service’s functional and disability-related emphasis.

When this service may fit

  • Pain is substantially limiting mobility or everyday tasks.
  • Disability, access or support needs make home assessment useful.
  • The person wants help with an active rehabilitation plan.
  • Current exercise or activity advice is difficult to put into practice.

An individual starting point

Build a plan the person can use

Functional practice

Break a chosen activity into manageable parts, such as standing to prepare food or walking a short familiar route. Practise movement, strength and mobility in ways that connect directly to the goal.

Pacing and activity planning

Find a workable balance between activity and recovery. Consider task length, position changes, rest and the demands of the whole day, especially where doing too much on one day makes the next days harder.

Graded activity and movement confidence

Where clinically appropriate and agreed, gradually explore activities that have become difficult or concerning. Steps are chosen collaboratively and adjusted to the response, health conditions and preferences; progress is not a fixed schedule to push through symptoms.

Practical self-management

Develop a simple plan for exercise, everyday movement and symptom fluctuations. Review what helped, what was difficult and what needs adapting. Active practice and usable strategies are central, rather than relying on passive treatment.

Make the plan fit the home and support available

A home visit can clarify why a task is difficult: furniture height, walking distance, the sequence of activities or the assistance available may all matter. See Mobile Physiotherapy for how assessment works.

Mobility aids or changes to task set-up may help participation when indicated. Physiotherapy advice is coordinated with occupational therapy for complex equipment, daily activity strategies or home modifications. With consent, carers and support workers can help carry over the agreed plan without taking over tasks unnecessarily.

Manage fluctuations and review the goal

Progress may be uneven. A flare-up plan can help identify temporary adjustments and when to seek advice. New or substantially different symptoms need assessment rather than being assumed to be the usual pain.

Reviews consider participation, activity tolerance, confidence and support needs alongside symptoms. The plan may be continued, simplified or changed according to what is useful and sustainable.

Working alongside pain and medical care

With consent, Tandem can liaise with the GP, pain service, occupational therapist, psychologist and other treating clinicians. Relevant diagnoses, precautions and previous advice inform rehabilitation.

Medical investigation and medication management remain with the treating medical team. Psychological therapy is a separate discipline; where distress, sleep or other concerns need further input, this is discussed respectfully with the person and appropriate clinicians.

Referrals and service area

Check whether mobile rehabilitation is appropriate

Useful referral details include the main activity limitation, relevant medical assessment, current supports, previous rehabilitation and the reason a home visit is needed. People may enquire directly; family or professional referrals require consent or appropriate authority.

Tandem considers visits across Western Sydney and The Hills District, including Penrith and nearby communities. Read Service Areas; clinical fit, location and current availability are confirmed before booking.

Private enquiries are welcome. Persistent pain does not automatically establish NDIS eligibility or funding for physiotherapy. For appropriate NDIS referrals, Tandem accepts self-managed and plan-managed participants only and is not NDIS-registered. Funding and any required approvals are checked before care starts.

Related mobility and disability support

When unsteadiness is also limiting activity, see falls risk physiotherapy. For pain occurring alongside a spinal cord injury, SCI physiotherapy describes the broader transfer, mobility and equipment considerations.
