NDIS clients we support

Tandem Physio is best suited to participants where function, safety, mobility, fatigue, respiratory risk or equipment use need to be understood in the person’s everyday environment.

  • Neurological disability including stroke, MND, multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury, traumatic brain injury and acquired brain injury.
  • Mobility decline, falls risk, transfer difficulty, deconditioning or reduced community access.
  • Complex disability presentations requiring support worker training, equipment review or multidisciplinary communication.
  • Participants requiring clear reports to support therapy planning, plan reassessment or funding decisions.

Funding accepted

Tandem Physio currently supports NDIS self-managed and plan-managed participants. NDIA-managed referrals can be discussed if registration requirements or referral pathways change in future.

Before commencing, we confirm available funding, service agreement requirements, travel expectations, reporting needs and consent for communication with relevant supports.

Common NDIS physiotherapy goals

Therapy is focused on measurable function and practical participation, not generic exercise alone.

Mobility and falls risk

Assessment and intervention for walking, balance, transfers, endurance, community mobility and safe movement routines.

Carer and support worker training

Education for transfer practice, positioning, equipment use, exercise support and escalation signs where clinically appropriate.

Equipment-linked rehabilitation

Functional input around mobility aids, standing programs, transfer equipment, positioning, wheelchair routines and trial recommendations.

Progressive conditions

Monitoring and adjustment of therapy goals for conditions where function, fatigue, respiratory status or support needs may change over time.

Functional reports

Clear documentation linking impairments, activity limitations, participation restrictions, risk and requested supports.

Care-team communication

Consent-based liaison with families, support coordinators, OTs, speech pathologists, GPs, specialists and support providers.

Referral information for support coordinators

Useful referral details include the participant’s suburb, funding type, relevant diagnoses, current functional concern, urgency, key risks, support arrangements and whether a specific report or therapy recommendation is required.
