Stroke rehabilitation · Western Sydney and The Hills

Stroke physiotherapy for the movements that matter at home

Getting out of a favourite chair, reaching the front gate or managing the bathroom can become rehabilitation goals after stroke. Tandem Physio provides mobile physiotherapy for adults rebuilding these skills or living with ongoing movement difficulties.

Different stages, different priorities

Early rehabilitation and life after discharge

Early rehabilitation often involves relearning movement and establishing routines after hospital. Home physiotherapy can build on the discharge plan when community care is appropriate, with attention to precautions, assistance and the rehabilitation already underway.

Longer-term goals may include improving a particular task, returning to an activity or maintaining the abilities that support everyday life. Progress varies; time since stroke alone does not define a person’s goals or potential. Review considers what is changing and whether the current plan remains useful.

Reasons to refer or review

  • Returning home with unresolved walking or transfer difficulties.
  • Falls, near misses or reduced confidence getting around.
  • A change in strength, endurance, movement or assistance needed.
  • Existing exercises no longer matching everyday goals.
  • Family or support workers needing clearer movement strategies.

Practice with a purpose

Connecting movement practice to everyday tasks

Assessment considers weakness, altered sensation, coordination, balance, fatigue and the level of support needed. The plan focuses on selected tasks rather than a standard exercise sheet.

Walking and balance

Practise stepping, turning and navigating household routes, with assistance or a walking aid where needed. Balance work may address reaching, changing direction and negotiating thresholds, alongside strategies to reduce falls risk.

Transfers and functional movement

Break down getting out of bed, standing from a chair or moving onto the toilet into manageable practice. Repetition, setup and the amount of help are adjusted to the person’s movement and safety needs.

Strength and activity tolerance

Build relevant strength and walking tolerance through appropriately dosed exercise. For example, supported sit-to-stand practice may contribute to a chair-transfer goal, with rest and review of fatigue or symptoms.

Mobility aids and family support

Review walking-aid fit, use and suitability in the actual environment. Agree practical ways for family or support workers to assist movement and support practice between visits without asking them to provide unsafe or unassessed assistance.

Why assess in the usual environment?

A chair’s height, the space beside a bed or the route to the letterbox can change how a task is managed. A home visit allows practice with the person’s own furniture, equipment and support, and helps identify which strategies are realistic between sessions.

Community assessment may be useful when goals extend to outdoor paths or local activities. The route, fatigue and available assistance are considered before progressing practice. See Mobile Physiotherapy for the assessment approach.

Joining up the rehabilitation plan

With consent, physiotherapy can coordinate with the stroke or rehabilitation team, GP, occupational therapist and other clinicians. Communication or swallowing concerns need speech pathology input; mood and adjustment concerns may need psychology or medical support.

Visual, perceptual or cognitive difficulties can affect movement practice and require team input. Orthotic, complex seating, assistive technology and home-modification needs are addressed with appropriately qualified professionals. Medical management remains with the treating medical team.

Referrals and service area

Arrange a stroke physiotherapy assessment

Adults, families, discharge teams, clinicians and support coordinators can enquire. Include the main task causing difficulty, current assistance, relevant discharge recommendations and suburb. Referrals on someone else’s behalf require consent or appropriate authority.

Home visits are considered across Western Sydney and The Hills District, including Penrith, Blacktown and Parramatta. See Service Areas; suitability, location and appointment availability are confirmed individually.

Tandem accepts self-managed and plan-managed NDIS physiotherapy enquiries and is not an NDIS-registered provider. Private enquiries are welcome. Funding arrangements and any approvals are confirmed before services start.
