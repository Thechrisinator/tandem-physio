Practical movement strategies for Parkinson’s disease and parkinsonism

When starting a step, turning or getting out of a chair takes more thought, physiotherapy can help work through the task. Tandem provides home and community visits for adults with Parkinson’s disease and other parkinsonian presentations.

Understanding the presentation

Similar movement difficulties, different needs

Parkinsonism describes a group of movement features; it is not another name for Parkinson’s disease. Different underlying conditions can produce these features, with different patterns of change and responses to treatment.

Slowness of movement (bradykinesia), smaller steps, stiffness or difficulty combining movements may affect daily life. Assessment starts with the person’s actual difficulties and priorities, rather than assuming that one exercise or cue will suit everyone.

Reasons to refer or review

  • Walking becomes slower, less steady or harder to start.
  • Freezing, difficult turns, near misses or falls occur.
  • Getting out of bed or a chair needs more help.
  • Exercise, equipment or support routines no longer fit.

Practice with a purpose

Working on movement that carries into the day

Mobile physiotherapy

Steps, turns and freezing

Observe gait and practise starts, stops and changes of direction. Where freezing is present, trial an individual visual, verbal or rhythmic cue and check whether it helps in the person’s usual setting.

Size and sequence of movement

Practise a purposeful movement amplitude, such as reaching further or taking a larger step when appropriate. Break tasks such as turning in bed or rising from a chair into manageable parts.

Balance, strength and attention

Choose strength and balance exercises matched to ability and falls risk. Assess what happens when walking and talking or carrying an item; dual-task practice is introduced only when suitable, with simpler strategies where needed.

Aids and support strategies

Trial mobility aids in context, considering turning, attention and freezing rather than assuming an aid will help. With consent, involve family or support workers in consistent cues, appropriate assistance and practice between visits.

Why the doorway or usual chair matters

A narrow doorway, a crowded room or a low lounge may reveal difficulties not seen on a clear clinic walkway. Home assessment allows a strategy to be tried where it is needed, using the person’s furniture and available support.

Community goals might include negotiating a path, approaching a seat or managing a familiar outing. The task, distance and assistance are selected together, without assuming that clinic performance will transfer automatically.

Adapting over time

Function may vary across the day, including around prescribed medication timing. This helps inform appointment timing and practice; medication changes remain with the prescriber. Review can shift the emphasis between physical capacity, movement strategies, maintenance and additional assistance as needs change.

A coordinated care plan

With consent, observations can be shared with the GP, neurologist, Parkinson’s nurse, occupational therapist and other clinicians. New or marked functional changes may need medical review.

Occupational therapy input may be needed for daily activities, home modifications or complex equipment. Speech pathology and medical teams address swallowing or communication concerns. Cognitive or mood difficulties may require additional input and adjustments to how physiotherapy is delivered.

Referrals and service area

Discuss the movement problem you are seeing

Individuals, families and treating teams can enquire. Useful details include the main movement difficulty, falls or freezing, current assistance, relevant diagnosis and usual symptom pattern. Referring on someone’s behalf requires consent or appropriate authority.

Visits are considered across Western Sydney and The Hills District, including Penrith and surrounding areas. See Service Areas; clinical fit, location and availability are confirmed before booking.

Private enquiries are welcome. For NDIS physiotherapy, Tandem accepts self-managed and plan-managed participants only and is not NDIS-registered. Funding and any required approvals are checked before services commence.
