Falls risk and mobility · Western Sydney and The Hills

Understand falls risk in the tasks that matter

A fall or near miss can change how someone moves around their home. Tandem provides mobile physiotherapy for adults with balance, mobility or disability-related concerns, working to reduce falls risk while supporting everyday participation.

A functional assessment

Look beyond a balance score

Falls often involve several interacting factors. Assessment explores what happened, where and when it occurred, the task being attempted and the support or equipment in use. A near miss can also identify a useful opportunity for review.

The physiotherapy component considers walking, balance, strength, transfers and activity tolerance. It sits within a multifactorial approach that identifies when medical, occupational therapy or other assessment is needed. Risk can be reduced, but falls cannot always be prevented.

See Tandem’s Clinical Focus and About Chris for the service’s clinical background.

A review may be useful when

  • There are falls, repeated stumbles or near misses.
  • Confidence has reduced and usual activities are avoided.
  • Transfers require more help or a walking aid seems unsuitable.
  • Mobility changes after illness or a change in support.

Assessment where it happens

From the bed to the bathroom and beyond

Mobile physiotherapy

Home visits allow the usual route, furniture and assistance to be considered together. Community assessment may also be useful when uneven paths, kerbs or longer distances are part of the concern.

Walking and balance

Assess gait, turning, foot clearance and balance during relevant tasks. Select supervised practice and a manageable home programme, with enough support to challenge movement safely.

Strength and transfers

Work on the physical capacity and technique needed to rise from the usual chair, move from bed or negotiate a step. Recommendations account for the available space and help required.

Mobility aids and fatigue

Check aid fit, use and suitability in the actual environment. Consider whether fatigue, rushing or the length of a task changes steadiness, and plan rest or assistance accordingly.

Environment and shared routines

Observe obstacles, access and lighting concerns that affect movement. With consent, practise agreed assistance with carers or support workers. Occupational therapy may be needed for a fuller environmental assessment, home modifications or complex equipment.

Address contributing factors with the right team

Dizziness, fainting, medicine effects, vision changes, footwear, continence needs and other health issues can influence falls risk. Physiotherapy observations help identify concerns; they do not replace assessment by the relevant clinician.

With consent, Tandem can liaise with the GP, occupational therapist, pharmacist, nursing team or other treating professionals. Medical causes and medicine reviews remain with the appropriate clinicians, and home-modification planning requires suitably qualified input.

A fall involving injury, fainting or a sudden change in health may need prompt medical assessment before routine rehabilitation. Tandem is not an emergency or post-fall response service.

Review the plan as circumstances change

Goals may include reaching the front gate, using the bathroom with appropriate support or returning to an outing. Progress is reviewed against these tasks, near misses and confidence, alongside physical measures.

A new aid, illness, altered routine or increased assistance can change the plan. Long-term management may combine ongoing exercise, practical adaptations and consistent support, rather than a single assessment.

Referrals and service area

Share the circumstances, not just the number of falls

A referral is more useful when it describes recent events, any injury or medical review, current mobility and assistance, and what the person wants to do more safely. Individuals and families may enquire directly; referrals on another person’s behalf need consent or appropriate authority.

Mobile appointments are considered across Western Sydney and The Hills District, including Penrith. See Service Areas for broader coverage; location, clinical suitability and current availability are checked individually.

Private referrals are welcome. NDIS physiotherapy is available to suitable self-managed and plan-managed participants only; Tandem is not NDIS-registered. Falls risk alone does not establish funding eligibility. Funding arrangements and approvals must be confirmed before services begin.

When a neurological condition also affects balance

For rehabilitation after a stroke, see stroke physiotherapy. Where freezing or difficulty initiating movement contributes, read about Parkinson’s disease and parkinsonism. Fluctuating mobility and fatigue are also considered in MS physiotherapy.
