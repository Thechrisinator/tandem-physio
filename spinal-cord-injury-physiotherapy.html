Spinal cord injury physiotherapy · Western Sydney and The Hills

Practical movement and transfer support after spinal cord injury

Tandem Physio provides mobile physiotherapy for adults with spinal cord injury, working on the movements, equipment and assistance that make everyday routines possible at home and in the community.

Different injuries, different priorities

Start with what the person needs to do

Injury level and completeness influence movement, sensation and support needs, but do not tell the whole story. Assessment also considers current abilities, medical precautions, previous rehabilitation, fatigue, environment and personal goals.

For one person, the priority may be moving from a wheelchair to bed with less effort. For another, it may be supported standing, walking practice or directing assistance more confidently. Independence can include choosing how care is provided.

Read About Chris and Tandem’s Clinical Focus to help assess clinical fit.

Why assess at home?

A transfer that works in rehabilitation may be harder beside the person’s own bed. Surface heights, space for a wheelchair or hoist, and the support available all affect the task.

A home visit allows these details to be assessed together. See Mobile Physiotherapy for the assessment approach.

Physiotherapy in daily routines

Build skills while managing physical demands

Transfers and bed mobility

Assess rolling, moving into sitting and transfers between usual surfaces. Practise suitable techniques, review assistance and transfer equipment, and help support workers follow an agreed approach.

Wheelchair function and upper-limb preservation

Consider wheelchair manoeuvring, reaching and propulsion where relevant. Review repeated loading during daily tasks, shoulder symptoms, strength and technique to support upper-limb preservation rather than simply adding more repetitions.

Strength, range and positioning

Develop movement and exercise around available muscle function and meaningful tasks. Joint range and supported positions are considered alongside comfort, skin precautions and the existing care plan.

Standing and walking where appropriate

Standing or gait practice depends on assessment, goals and medical suitability, including weight-bearing restrictions and blood-pressure tolerance. It is not an assumed goal or a promise of walking recovery.

After discharge and over time

Revisit routines before they become unsustainable

Rehabilitation after returning home may focus on applying newly learnt skills. Years after injury, review may instead address shoulder loading, reduced stamina, altered support or a different home setup.

Consider referral when transfers need more assistance, wheelchair tasks become painful or tiring, joint movement limits care, or support workers need practical training. A change in equipment or an interruption to usual activity may also prompt review.

Recommendations may include adjusting a task, changing the exercise dose or seeking additional equipment assessment. New or unexplained changes need appropriate medical review, rather than being assumed to reflect deconditioning.

Equipment and team responsibilities

Physiotherapy contributes information about movement, transfers and physical capacity. Complex seating, wheelchair configuration, assistive technology and home modifications require collaboration with OT and relevant seating or equipment services.

Pressure-injury concerns need timely nursing or medical input and coordination with the seating team. Physiotherapy positioning advice complements that plan; it does not replace wound management.

Coordinate with the spinal and rehabilitation team

With consent, Chris can liaise with the spinal service, rehabilitation physician, GP, OT, nursing team and case manager. Medical management of spinal cord injury complications remains with the appropriate treating team.

Where breathing or cough function affects activity or daily care, respiratory physiotherapy is considered only within clinician competence, clinical suitability and the agreed care plan. Respiratory assessment, ventilation and medical decisions require the relevant respiratory or spinal team.

Refer for community spinal cord injury physiotherapy

Participants, families, rehabilitation teams and case managers can enquire. Include the main functional goal, current transfers and mobility, relevant precautions, suburb and funding pathway. Referral on someone else’s behalf requires consent or appropriate authority.

Visits are considered across Western Sydney and The Hills, including Penrith. Check Service Areas; suitability, travel and availability are confirmed individually.

iCare / LTCS enquiries are subject to relevant scheme approval where required. For NDIS physiotherapy, Tandem accepts self-managed and plan-managed participants only and is not NDIS-registered. Private enquiries are welcome; funding and approvals are confirmed before services start.
