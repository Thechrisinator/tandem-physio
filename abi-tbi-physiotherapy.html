ABI and TBI physiotherapy · Western Sydney and The Hills

Brain injury rehabilitation in everyday settings

After an acquired brain injury, moving around home or returning to community activities may take more effort, planning and support. Tandem Physio works with adults whose physical function has been affected, including people living with traumatic brain injury.

Understanding the person’s pattern of difficulty

Acquired brain injury (ABI) is an umbrella term that includes traumatic brain injury (TBI) and non-traumatic injuries, such as stroke or injury caused by a lack of oxygen. Physical effects vary: some people need assistance with basic transfers; others walk independently but struggle with balance, coordination or endurance in busy environments.

Assessment starts with what the person wants to do and what currently gets in the way. A short walk in a quiet room may not show the demands of getting to a local activity, managing uneven ground or moving when fatigued.

For Tandem’s approach and clinician background, see Clinical Focus and About Chris.

Making sessions workable

Memory, attention, communication, behaviour and executive-function difficulties can influence instructions, consent, pacing and safety. Sessions may use shorter tasks, fewer distractions or agreed prompts, guided by the person’s preferences and the treating team.

These considerations shape physiotherapy delivery. Cognitive rehabilitation, communication treatment and behaviour support require the relevant multidisciplinary clinicians. Physiotherapy works alongside their care.

Physical rehabilitation

From movement capacity to participation

Gait, balance and motor control

Assess stepping, turning, coordination and the ability to control movement during everyday tasks. Practice can progress from a predictable home route to more demanding settings when appropriate, with falls risk and supervision considered throughout.

Transfers and mobility equipment

Review bed mobility, standing up and movement between surfaces, including assistance and equipment. Walking aids may be trialled where suitable. Complex seating and other assistive technology needs are coordinated with occupational therapists and relevant services.

Strength, endurance and fatigue

Use functional strengthening and activity practice to address physical capacity. Session length, rest breaks and exercise dose account for fatigue and the rest of the person’s day; more activity is not automatically better.

Carrying strategies into daily life

With consent, involve carers or support workers in agreed transfer, walking and exercise routines. Clear instructions about assistance, prompts and when to stop can help the plan remain usable between appointments and across different workers.

Assessment where the difficulty occurs

A home visit can reveal how furniture, access routes and support routines affect movement. Community assessment may explore a meaningful route or activity, including distance, surfaces, distractions and opportunities to rest.

For example, a goal of joining a local group may involve practising the walk from transport to the venue, identifying the assistance needed and building tolerance gradually. This is physical rehabilitation within a wider participation plan, with other barriers discussed with the relevant team.

When a fresh review may help

  • A transition from hospital rehabilitation to home.
  • Falls, near misses or difficulty in busier settings.
  • Fatigue limiting physical activity or community access.
  • New goals, equipment or changes in support workers.
  • Declining movement or a programme that is no longer practical.

Continuity and team input

Reviewing function as life changes

Rehabilitation needs may continue after the initial recovery period. Goals can shift towards maintaining mobility, returning to activities or adapting to changes in health, home or support. Review is based on function and priorities rather than a promised recovery timeline.

With consent, Chris can communicate with rehabilitation physicians, brain injury services, GPs, occupational therapists, speech pathologists, psychologists or neuropsychologists, case managers and other treating professionals. Swallowing, cognition, communication, psychological and medical concerns require their respective clinicians. Home modifications and complex equipment planning require appropriately qualified input.

Referring for ABI or TBI physiotherapy

Enquiries can come from the person, family, rehabilitation team or case manager. Include current physical difficulties, relevant precautions, communication or support needs, suburb and funding pathway. Consent or appropriate authority is needed when referring on someone else’s behalf.

Visits are considered across Western Sydney and The Hills District, including Penrith, Parramatta and Blacktown. Check Service Areas; clinical fit and current availability are confirmed before an appointment.

Tandem is not NDIS-registered and accepts self-managed and plan-managed NDIS physiotherapy enquiries only. iCare / LTCS enquiries are subject to relevant scheme requirements and approval where required. Private enquiries are also welcome; funding is confirmed before services commence.
