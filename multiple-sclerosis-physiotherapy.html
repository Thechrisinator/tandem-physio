Multiple sclerosis physiotherapy · Western Sydney and The Hills

Physiotherapy that adapts to daily life with multiple sclerosis

When walking, energy or balance varies, a useful plan needs room to change. Tandem Physio offers home and community-based physiotherapy for adults with MS, shaped around current function and the activities they want to keep doing.

Understanding variable capacity

Plan for more than a good day

MS may affect strength, coordination, sensation, balance and energy in different combinations. Some people experience fluctuating symptoms; others have a more progressive change in function. The same diagnosis does not mean the same exercise programme or mobility plan.

Assessment considers what happens across the day, after activity and in different conditions. Walking to the letterbox may be manageable in the morning but leave too little energy for an afternoon appointment.

Physiotherapy aims to support function, activity and participation. It does not treat the underlying disease or replace neurological care. Read About Chris and the broader Clinical Focus.

What a home visit can reveal

Distances between rooms, stairs, chair heights and the timing of usual routines can change the effort a task requires. Assessment can identify where practice, an aid or a rest break would be useful.

The Mobile Physiotherapy approach also considers the person’s available support and preferred community activities.

Movement with a purpose

Connect exercise to everyday tasks

Walking, balance and falls risk

Assess gait, turning, foot clearance and stability during relevant tasks. Practice might involve navigating a doorway, managing a step or choosing a more suitable mobility aid, with support matched to the person’s safety needs.

Strength and activity tolerance

Choose strengthening and aerobic activity that fits current capacity. Session length, repetitions, resistance and recovery are adjusted according to symptoms and the effect on later activities, rather than progressed automatically.

Transfers and comfortable movement

Work on rising from a usual chair, getting into bed or moving with assistance. Where stiffness limits tasks, review range, positioning and movement strategies alongside the treating team’s management.

Equipment that fits changing needs

Assess and trial walking aids where appropriate. A person may need different options for short indoor distances and longer outings. Wheelchair, complex seating or home-modification needs are addressed with OT and relevant services.

Fatigue and symptom sensitivity

Make the plan achievable across the week

Pacing can include dividing a demanding task, alternating activity and recovery, or scheduling exercise around essential daily commitments. The aim is a workable level of activity, with enough flexibility for days when symptoms are more limiting.

For people sensitive to heat, session timing, a cooler space and planned breaks may help make activity more manageable. Exercise choices and dose are reviewed against the person’s response, including how they feel afterwards.

With permission, family or support workers can learn the agreed routine, assistance and reasons to pause or seek advice. Brief, practical instructions help a plan carry over between visits.

When change needs wider review

New or sustained worsening should be discussed with the MS or medical team. Changes should not simply be attributed to heat, fatigue or lack of exercise.

Physiotherapy can document the functional impact and adapt activity while medical assessment is arranged. Relapse assessment, medication and disease-modifying treatment decisions remain with the treating medical team.

Review when the current approach stops fitting

Referral may be useful after falls or near misses, reduced walking distance, difficulty getting out of a chair, or fatigue that disrupts essential routines. Another reason is uncertainty about returning to activity after a period of reduced function.

Longer-term management may balance skill and strength work with maintenance, equipment and increased assistance. Goals are revisited as circumstances change; a progressive presentation does not remove the value of practical functional support.

With consent, Chris can coordinate with the neurologist, MS nurse, GP and OT. Cognition or mood concerns may need neuropsychology or psychology input; communication and swallowing concerns require speech pathology and medical assessment. Physiotherapy recommendations sit alongside these roles.

Arrange an MS physiotherapy suitability check

Enquiries can come from the person, family, treating team or support coordinator. Useful details include usual mobility, how symptoms vary, recent falls or functional changes, suburb and funding pathway. Consent or appropriate authority is needed when referring on another person’s behalf.

Home visits are considered across Western Sydney and The Hills, including Penrith. See Service Areas and confirm location, clinical suitability and appointment availability.

Tandem accepts self-managed and plan-managed participants for NDIS physiotherapy and is not an NDIS-registered provider. Private enquiries are also welcome. Funding arrangements and any approvals are checked before services commence.
